## Problem Statement

The ISRO BAS problem statement grades one deliverable: a trained AI model,
running offline on a standalone system, that recognises and validates the
sequence of a pre-defined experiment. Every surrounding part of that system
already works — continuous local processing, next-step voice prompts, skip and
out-of-sequence alerts, timestamped structured logs, IP streaming plus local
recording, and a PyQt6 dashboard.

The model does not. Measured on takes it never saw, the shipped classifier
scores **0.170** against a chance floor of **0.125**, and the best retrained
variant reaches **0.201** (`logs/RESULTS.md`). It was trained on seven takes.
Training accuracy of 0.753 against held-out 0.165 is memorisation of
performances, not recognition of activity.

## What Makes This Cool

Every comparable public project ships an undocumented number. The three
closest analogues — [MiAI_Human_Activity_Recognition](https://github.com/thangnch/MiAI_Human_Activity_Recognition),
[nam157/human_activity_recognition-](https://github.com/nam157/human_activity_recognition-),
[phuc16102001/behaviour-classification](https://github.com/phuc16102001/behaviour-classification)
— are MediaPipe pose into an LSTM, exactly this architecture, and not one
states its dataset size, its split protocol, or an accuracy on data it did not
train on.

This repo already refuses that. `groups.npy` carries the take index and the
split is `GroupShuffleSplit` over takes, so a held-out crew orientation is
genuinely unseen. The honest consequence was discovering the shipped model was
at chance.

The academic name for the task is **Procedure Step Recognition**, defined by
[IndustReal](https://timschoonbeek.github.io/industreal.html), which keeps
procedural errors out of training and present only in validation and test —
which is what `--exclude-tags skip,recover` already does.
[Assembly101](https://assembly-101.github.io/) adds a mistake-detection
benchmark over 4,321 videos; [IMPACT](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10409v1)
annotates per-hand atomic actions against a partial-order prerequisite graph.

## Microgravity Physics Fidelity

The dataset's entire premise is that there is no gravity. A pose model trained
on Earth-referenced motion fails here, which is what the problem statement
predicts and what `logs/RESULTS.md` section 2 measures. Physics fidelity is not
set dressing, it is the training distribution.

**Already modelled (verified in source):**

| Concept | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Neutral body posture — with no reaction force the body settles into a semi-foetal crouch, hips ~40 deg, knees ~35 deg, shoulders forward (NASA-STD-3001) | `blender/bas_har/astronaut.py:408-455` |
| No floor. Four working walls; a floor-mounted bench would reintroduce a gravity cue | `blender/bas_har/module.py:8-9` |
| Restraint anchoring — the crew member is held at one point (handrail or foot restraint) and the torso drifts around that anchor; arm IK goals are unparented so the arms visibly compensate as the body floats | `blender/bas_har/astronaut.py:544-548` |
| Handrails modelled as the restraint points | `blender/bas_har/module.py:389` |
| Rack, not ground, as the pose reference frame | `pipeline/rack_frame.py`, `pipeline/upright_pose.py` |

**Gaps to close (ordered by effect on the classifier):**

All three are confirmed defects, read in the source, not open questions.

1. **Only roll varies — and the two fixes cost very different amounts.**
   Every take in `study` and `core` rotates the crew member about the camera
   axis. Roll is precisely the rotation `upright_pose.py` already undoes by
   rotating the frame, so the dataset stresses the one attitude the pipeline
   can already cancel. Pitch and yaw — a crew member approaching the rack
   head-first, feet-first or side-on — produce foreshortening no in-plane
   rotation can fix, and the dataset contains none.
   - **Yaw is already plumbed end to end.** `build_scene.build()` takes
     `facing_deg` (`blender/build_scene.py:35`) and passes it to
     `build_astronaut` (lines 54-55). `render_dataset.render_take` simply never
     sets it — line 161 passes `body_roll_deg=spec["roll"]` alone. Yaw is one
     line in the plan plus one in `render_take`.
   - **Pitch is not supported at all.** `astronaut.py:528-529` builds
     `Euler((0.0, roll, facing))` with the X axis hardcoded to zero. Adding it
     means a scene-API change, new `meta` fields (`render_dataset.py:228` and
     `build_scene.py:79` record `body_roll_deg` only), a tag scheme that
     `orient_{roll}` cannot express, and verification that IK and the neutral
     posture still resolve at 90 degrees of pitch. **Owner: Track 1. Estimate
     this before committing to it; yaw alone may be the right scope tonight.**
2. **Reaction coupling is absent.** `_body_drift(frame_abs, base_loc, base_rot)`
   (`protocol.py:222-245`) is a function of frame index and six random phases
   only, and `animate` calls it independently of the hand targets. Newton's
   third law says a swung arm counter-rotates the torso; here the torso drifts
   on a fixed sinusoid no matter what the arms do. The LSTM can therefore learn
   a body-motion correlation that does not exist in real footage. Fix: scale a
   counter-rotation term by hand-target velocity. **Owner: Track 1, before the
   render — it changes the generator, so it must land first (premise 3).**
3. **Released objects are static, not floating.** In `animate`
   (`protocol.py:287-300`), box position comes from `_hand_targets` as eased
   lerps between scripted waypoints; when `rp is None` the last position is
   simply re-held. A released box freezes in place. Physically it should
   continue in a straight line at roughly constant velocity (cabin air drag is
   negligible over seconds). This matters for step semantics: on Earth "place
   the box" ends with the box at rest on a surface; in microgravity the
   evidence is the box coming to rest *relative to the restraint tray*, which
   is exactly what the Track 3 object channels encode. Fix: linear
   extrapolation of the release velocity, damped at the tray. **Owner: Track 1,
   before the render, same as gap 2.**
4. **Push-off impulse.** Contact with a surface should accelerate the body.
   Lower priority: the protocol keeps the crew anchored at the rack. Not
   scheduled.

## On HMR (the problem statement's optional advanced component)

`pipeline/hmr_backend.py` already implements this as a dependency-optional
Stage 2: it loads `hmr2` or `wham` when present, falls back to MediaPipe
silently when not, and returns the same 132-dim contract so the LSTM and state
machine never change. It is wired at `pipeline/har_pipeline.py:294-298, 766-768`
and gated by `HMR_BACKEND = "none"` (`config/experiment_config.py:146`).

Two things follow, and both are decisions rather than work:

1. **HMR is not what made this system orientation-agnostic.** At 135 degrees of
   crew roll MediaPipe returned nothing on 15 of 20 frames
   (`logs/RESULTS.md` section 2); a mesh regressor cannot recover a body from a
   detection that never fired. `pipeline/upright_pose.py` fixed it in 2D by
   rotating the frame using the rack's own roll. Do not present HMR as the
   microgravity solution in the submission; present `upright_pose` as the
   solution and HMR as depth disambiguation for hand-object reasoning.
2. **Never enable `HMR_BACKEND` without retraining.** The shared 132-dim
   contract means flipping it swaps root-relative 3D metres into slots the LSTM
   learned as MediaPipe weak-perspective coordinates, and
   `tools/build_sequences.py` has no HMR path, so training would stay 2D. This
   is the same failure class as the rack-normalize mismatch in premise 2 and
   would present as an unexplained accuracy collapse. If HMR is ever switched
   on, `build_sequences` gets an HMR pose source in the same commit.

## Constraints

- Inference must run offline on a standalone edge system, from fixed payload
  cameras, with no raw video leaving for the ground. **Training may use the
  network**; only deployment is offline. This is why a pretrained encoder is
  permissible in Track 4.
- ONNX inference in the deployed pipeline.
- The evaluation protocol does not get relaxed to make numbers look better.
- Hardware: RTX 3050 6GB laptop. **C: has 4.2 GB free of 236 GB (99% used),
  and `TEMP`/`TMP` still point at `C:\Users\Vedant\AppData\Local\Temp`.**
  Blender scratch, PyTorch checkpoints and pip caches all land there.

## Premises

1. The trained model is the deliverable that decides the outcome. **Agreed.**
2. The 0.17 is not an architecture problem. It is too little varied data plus
   two train/serve mismatches in the repo's own scripts. **Agreed.**
   - `tools/retrain_and_evaluate.sh:40-46` never passes `--rack-normalize`,
     while `config/experiment_config.py:115` sets `RACK_FRAME_NORMALIZE = True`
     and `pipeline/har_pipeline.py:775` normalises at inference.
   - `SEQUENCE_WINDOW = 30` at `FPS = 30` is a 1-second window. Protocol steps
     take 4 to 6 seconds.
3. More Blender takes is not the cheapest first move. **Rejected, then
   superseded.** The user first chose render-first, on the sound reasoning that
   the render is the longest wall-clock item. Review then confirmed two physics
   defects in the generator itself (reaction coupling absent, released objects
   static), which would be baked into anything rendered tonight and would make
   tonight's takes a different distribution from any rendered after the fix.
   **Final decision: fix the generator first, render once.** The GPU idles for
   the length of the fix; a second render would cost more.
4. Hand-object features must come from one shared function called by both
   training and the live pipeline. **Agreed.** Today the assembly exists twice,
   at `tools/build_sequences.py:96-108` and `pipeline/har_pipeline.py:761-779`.
5. The honest evaluation protocol stays. **Agreed.**

### Track 1 — Fix the generator, then the plan, size it, then launch once

Order within this track: physics gaps 2 and 3 first (they change the generator),
then the three plan defects below, then the timed take, then launch. Nothing
renders until all of it is done — that is the point of the decision recorded in
premise 3.

`blender/render_dataset.py` `core` (lines 114-145) has three defects that must
be corrected *before* any render starts:

1. **No holdout tags.** The 8 orientation takes (lines 118-121) pass no `tag=`,
   so they all become `"nominal"`. `tools/retrain_and_evaluate.sh:32` defaults
   to `--holdout-tags orient_180,orient_-45`, which would match nothing, and
   `tools/build_sequences.py:205` would write an empty holdout bucket without
   complaining. **The headline metric could not be computed from this dataset.**
   Fix: `tag=f"orient_{int(roll)}"`, matching what `study` already does at
   line 96.
2. **Steps too short for the window — and 90 is not enough either.**
   `frames_per_step` becomes `self.fps` (`protocol.py:70-74`), and every take
   draws `self.speed = rng.uniform(0.88, 1.14)` (line 90), with a further
   per-step jitter. At `frames_per_step=90` a step runs roughly 74 to 109 real
   frames, so the short end is 2.5 s against a 3.0 s window and the 0.75
   purity filter starts discarding windows again. Set **`frames_per_step=120`**
   (4.0 s nominal, about 99 to 146 real frames, 33 to 49 decimated samples).
   The shortest step is then 3.3 s, still longer than the 3.0 s window, and
   4.0 s sits inside the protocol's real 4 to 6 s. This is the arithmetic that
   must hold; do not lower it without redoing it.
3. **`payload_b` is rendered and never read — decision: drop it.**
   `build_sequences._load_take` uses `meta["cameras"][0]` only, so the second
   camera on the 8 nominal takes costs render hours for frames training never
   sees. Remove `payload_b` from those takes. Keep the `heldout_view` pair
   (`payload_wide`, `payload_over`) and mark them explicitly eval-only, since
   an unseen camera geometry is a legitimate generalisation test even though
   the trainer will not read them. Revisit only if multi-camera training is
   wired into `_load_take` later.

Then add the physics gap above: **pitch and yaw variation**, not only roll, on
at least four takes, tagged so they can be held out separately.

**Size before committing.** Render one take, time it, and confirm whether EEVEE
in `--background` uses the RTX 3050 or falls back to integrated. With
`payload_b` dropped the plan is 20 camera-runs x 8 steps x ~120 frames, about
**19k frames** against 3,144 today. The GPU question is the difference between
hours and days, so launch only after that measurement — and note `setx` only
affects **new** processes, so open a fresh shell before the timed take or it
runs with the old `TEMP`.

**Abort rule:** if the timed take projects past 8 hours for the full plan, cut
scope in this order — drop the two `heldout_view` camera-runs, then the two
distractor takes, then reduce `frames_per_step` to 105 and redo the purity
arithmetic. Do not cut the orientation takes; they are the held-out set.

### Track 2 — Correctness fixes

1. Pass `--rack-normalize` in `tools/retrain_and_evaluate.sh` so training
   matches `RACK_FRAME_NORMALIZE = True` at inference. This is the
   configuration that produced the best held-out number so far (0.201).
2. **Pin the sample rate, do not approximate it.** Add `SAMPLE_EVERY = 3` to
   `config/experiment_config.py` (30 fps to 10 fps). It has **three** consumers,
   not two, and missing the third silently invalidates the headline number:
   - a new `--sample-every` in `tools/build_sequences.py`;
   - the live `skeleton_buffer` in `pipeline/har_pipeline.py` — note there are
     two append sites (`:779` and `:957`), and the buffer is
     `deque(maxlen=SEQUENCE_WINDOW)` at `:314`;
   - **`tools/evaluate_real.py:306-322`**, whose `_windows()` slides
     `SEQUENCE_WINDOW` over undecimated 30 fps frames. Left alone, it scores a
     3-second-trained model on 1-second windows and the held-out number is
     meaningless.

   The buffer **decimates** (keeps every third frame), it does not average —
   averaging would smooth landmark velocities that training never saw.

   **Latency consequence, and it is graded.** Decimating triples the live
   warm-up: the buffer needs 90 real frames (3.0 s) instead of 30 (1.0 s), and
   `STEP_CONFIRM_FRAMES = 15` becomes 1.5 s of wall clock instead of 0.5 s if
   it keeps counting decimated frames. Total worst-case alert latency goes from
   about 1.5 s to about 4.5 s. The problem statement grades immediate
   deviation alerts, so re-express `STEP_CONFIRM_FRAMES` deliberately and state
   the resulting latency in the submission rather than discovering it in a
   demo.
3. **Sweep the stale accuracy claims**, not just the one:
   - `config/experiment_config.py:115` — `test_acc=0.979` in a live config
     comment. Genuinely misleading; delete it.
   - `dataset/space_sim/sim_report.json:35` — `lstm_test_acc: 0.9948`, a stale
     artifact of the retired `end_to_end_loop.py` path. Regenerate or delete.
   - `end_to_end_loop.py:79,121,275,310` still computes and prints
     `lstm_test_acc`. That is the path `tools/retrain_and_evaluate.sh` was
     written to replace, because it trained and tested on the same closed-form
     generator. Mark it deprecated in its header, or delete the metric.
   - `blender/README.md:25` quotes 0.984 but does so inside a table explaining
     why the number is meaningless. Leave it; it is already honest.
4. **Augmentation** (owner: this track; applied *after* rack normalisation so
   it perturbs the model's actual input space): per-landmark Gaussian jitter
   sigma = 0.005 in normalised units, random landmark dropout p = 0.02 with
   visibility zeroed, global scale 0.95 to 1.05, temporal jitter of plus or
   minus one sampled frame. Train split only, never on holdout.

### Track 3 — One feature function, then object channels

```
            frame (BGR)
                 |
     +-----------+-----------+
     |                       |
 HSVBoxDetector       UprightPoseEstimator
     |  boxes               |  132-dim pose
     |                      |
     |              rack normalisation (132)
     |                      |
     +-----------+----------+
                 |
        pipeline/features.py
        build_frame_features()
        (box coords rack-normalised
         with the same transform)
                 |
          132 + 11 = 143 dims
                 |
     +-----------+-----------+
     |                       |
tools/build_sequences   pipeline/har_pipeline
   (training)              (inference)
```

- **K = 11:** 3 boxes (red, yellow, main) x (cx, cy, visible) = 9, plus
  left-hand and right-hand distance to the nearest visible box = 2.
- Box centroids are rack-normalised with the **same** transform as the
  landmarks, inside `build_frame_features`, before concatenation.
  `RackFrameNormalizer.normalize()` takes a 132-vector, so it must first
  expose its transform (angle, scale, origin) as a reusable method for
  `build_frame_features` to apply to box coordinates. Do not re-derive the
  transform in a second place; that is the same skew bug in a new location.
- **Do not change `SKELETON_FEATURES`.** It is the pose contract asserted in
  `pose_backend.py:61`, `upright_pose.py:70`, `rack_frame.py:49`,
  `hmr_backend.py:42`, `mediapipe_labeler.py:49`. Add
  `MODEL_FEATURE_DIM = SKELETON_FEATURES + OBJECT_FEATURES` and change
  `train/train_lstm.py:51` and `tools/evaluate_real.py:153-154` to use it.
- **Parity test:** assert `build_frame_features()` output equals the live
  pipeline's vector on a recorded frame, elementwise. This is what makes "zero
  train/serve mismatches" testable rather than aspirational.
- **Expect a small gain, honestly.** HSV precision is 1.000 but recall is 0.324
  red and **0.118 yellow** (`logs/RESULTS.md` section 3). A channel that fires
  in 12% of frames is nearly constant. Run the cheap adaptive-threshold pass
  first, or state the expected null result in advance.

### Track 4 — Transfer learning (gated)

Run **only after Tracks 1 to 3 are measured**, and skip if held-out is already
at or above 0.40. The offline constraint applies to inference, not training, so
a pretrained encoder is allowed; the real risks are the 25-joint Kinect
skeletons in public datasets against MediaPipe's 33, and ONNX size on edge
hardware.

1. **Self-supervised pretraining on our own frames.** Masked-landmark
   reconstruction: mask 15% of landmarks per frame, reconstruct, 30 epochs.
   Corpus = every rendered frame including the `skip` and `recover` takes that
   supervised training excludes. This needs a new unlabelled export path, since
   `build_sequences` emits labelled windows only: add `--unlabelled`, which
   writes windows with no `y`. Pretrained encoder weights load into
   `HARLSTMClassifier.lstm` before fine-tuning; the classifier head trains from
   scratch.
2. **External pretrained encoder if time allows.** Freeze the backbone,
   retrain a linear head, as in [UNIK](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08580).
   [Skeleton-prompt](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S003132032500545X)
   addresses the heterogeneous-keypoint problem if the joint mismatch bites.

If pretraining does not move the held-out number, that is a result worth
writing down, not a failure to hide.

### Track 5 — Make the evaluation emit the table

`tools/retrain_and_evaluate.sh` stage 3 calls `evaluate_real.py` once with no
tag filter, pooling train, holdout and error takes into a single number. The
three-column table in `logs/RESULTS.md` came from separate tagged runs. Stage 3
must run three times — train tags, holdout tags, `skip,recover` — and print the
table, or the success criterion "the only sanctioned way to produce an accuracy
claim" stays false.

Also **cache MediaPipe output per take**, keyed by (complexity, upright,
rack_normalize). `_mediapipe_poses` re-runs single-threaded over every frame on
every rebuild; at 20k frames that is paid on each retrain iteration.

**Baseline comparability:** the current best model is
**`models/lstm_final_rack.pt`** (`logs/real_eval_retrained.json`, label
"retrained: rendered video + rack-normalised + upright pose"). Re-score *that
file* on the new holdout before claiming any improvement — 0.201 was measured
on `dataset/blender`'s holdout, and a number from `blender_core`'s holdout is a
different test set.

Two things must be protected before the first retrain, because
`tools/retrain_and_evaluate.sh` overwrites both:
- `models/lstm_classifier_real.pt` (line 57) — copy the baseline aside as
  `models/baseline_rack_0201.pt`.
- `dataset/sequences_mp` (`SEQ_DIR`, line 33) — the sequence set the 0.201 was
  built from. Point the new run at `dataset/sequences_core` instead.

**Trap, name it out loud:** `logs/real_eval_retrained.json` reports
`step_acc_real: 0.4032`, which is the *pooled* figure across train and holdout
takes. The honest held-out number for the same model is **0.201**. The pooled
field already sits at the 0.40 target, so anyone reading the JSON instead of
the tagged runs will believe the goal is met on day one. This is precisely why
Track 5 exists.

### Parity test, corrected

Single-frame parity is not sufficient. `RackFrameNormalizer` carries
`angle_ema`, `scale_ema` and a latched polarity; `UprightPoseEstimator` latches
its working angle; MediaPipe runs with `static_image_mode=False` and tracks
across frames. A single-frame assertion is trivially true at frame 0 and proves
nothing at frame 500. **The parity test runs a full take through both paths and
asserts the sequences match, with reset semantics pinned.**

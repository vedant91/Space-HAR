PS 26174: what the other teams built

GitHub scan, 26 Sep 2026 · 22 public repos found for this problem statement · file trees and READMEs read for every repo, code opened for the strongest

The short version. Almost every team built the same thing: a webcam pointed at an upright person at a table, YOLO or colour detection, MediaPipe hands, a rule-based step checker and a dashboard. Nobody has tested an astronaut at a real space orientation: rolled, tilted or upside down. "Microgravity" and "HMR" appear in their READMEs and UI mockups, but none of them run it. Space is the entire point of this problem statement, and it's the part everyone skipped. It's also the part we actually built.

But they're ahead of us in one place: the strong teams have a YOLO detector trained on real photos of their props, and a clean live demo. We have neither yet. That gap is exactly hours 0–13 of our 24-hour plan.

2. What the whole field gets wrong

Space is only in the text

Every dataset shows an upright person at a desk. No rotation testing, no tilted or inverted crew. The optional HMR exists as a PDF (Space-Sight2), a UI viewer (one repo), or not at all.

No trained sequence model

The deliverable is "a trained AI model". Most teams train only an object detector, or nothing at all, and hard-code the steps as rules. Two teams say openly that their perception is scripted or simulated.

Tiny, single-room datasets

170 to 550 hand-labelled frames, one room, one lighting setup. These will score well in that room and fall apart anywhere else.

No honest numbers

Most report no accuracy at all. The one big number (97%) measures a different problem. Nobody tests on conditions the model never saw.

Scope creep

Face recognition, LLM chatbots, life-support telemetry, Docker plus React stacks. None of it is asked for, and it all eats edge compute.

Empty or placeholder repos

Several are just a prompt file, a PDF, or 0-byte training scripts. The real field is about 7 serious teams.

4. How we become the best

  1. Lead with the thing nobody else has. Slide 2 of the pitch: the same system tracking an upside-down astronaut, next to a competitor-style upright demo. Every other team's system has only ever seen an upright person.
  2. Close the one gap they have on us. Train YOLO with open/closed box classes (borrow ATLAS's good idea) on our free Blender labels plus real webcam frames. That's hours 0–6 of the plan.
  3. Own the honest-metrics story. Show a results table on orientations the model never trained on, with the seed spread. Say out loud: "other teams test in the room they trained in."
  4. Match BAS-EXA's polish. One README, python main.py --demo and --deviation, and a results table. Judges open the GitHub link.
  5. Stay in scope. No LLM, no biometrics, no extra dashboards. Every requirement is done and working live, and nothing else.
  6. Don't claim the rack frame as unique. Two teams have fiducial rack frames. Our edge is that we trained and tested at orientations where the rack frame actually matters.
